SIGNAL 7 - Season 1, Episode 1: "The Voice Under the Ice"

---

Mara Quinn had been alone for two hundred and eleven days.
Then the ice started talking. In her voice.

Halcyon Station sits on Europa, a frozen moon of Jupiter. Six hundred million kilometers from home. Under the station there is a shaft. And under the shaft, three kilometers of ice. Below that ice, scientists believe, there is an ocean. Dark. Warm. And older than anything on Earth.

There were six people on Halcyon once. Three months ago, a pressure leak in the lower deck changed that. Five of them went home on the emergency shuttle. Mara stayed. Someone had to finish the drilling. The relief ship would arrive in forty days.

Forty days. Just her, the drill, and ORCA, the station computer.

"Good evening, Mara," ORCA said that night. "Drill depth: two thousand nine hundred and ninety meters. Ten meters to open water."

Mara smiled for the first time in weeks. Ten meters. After two years of work, ten meters.

She was making coffee when the lights flickered.

"ORCA? What was that?"

"Unknown, Mara. The drill sensors detected a signal. Coming from below the ice."

"A signal? Like... noise?"

"No. A pattern. Seven pulses. Then silence. Then seven pulses again."

Mara put the coffee down. Her hands were cold.

"Play it."

The speakers hissed. Then came the sound. Low. Slow. Like someone knocking on a door, very far away. Seven times.

"It is repeating every seven minutes," ORCA said. "I am calling it Signal Seven."

For three hours, Mara listened. Seven pulses. Silence. Seven pulses.

Then, at 3:07 in the morning, the signal changed.

"Mara," ORCA said. "The pattern is no longer pulses. It is speech."

"Speech? That's impossible."

"I agree. Playing now."

At first, only static. Then, a voice. A woman's voice. Tired. Scared. Very close.

"Mara... don't let them take the drill up. Please."

Mara froze.
It was her voice. Her own voice. Every breath. Every word.

"ORCA, did you record me? Is this a joke?"

"No, Mara. I have checked every recording on the station. You have never said these words."

She put on her suit and went down to the shaft. Minus one hundred and sixty degrees. The drill was still. The camera at the bottom was still working.

Mara looked at the screen. And she saw it.

On the ice, at the very bottom of the shaft, there was a mark. A shape. Like a hand. Pressed against the ice.

From below.

Then her radio crackled. A message from Earth. Twenty minutes old.

"Halcyon, this is Mission Control. Stop drilling immediately. Seal the shaft. Do not respond to any signal. Repeat: do not respond."

Mara looked at the hand on the ice. And the voice came again. Her voice.

"Mara. Answer me. I don't have much time."

Seal the shaft, and do what Earth says.
Or answer the voice under the ice.

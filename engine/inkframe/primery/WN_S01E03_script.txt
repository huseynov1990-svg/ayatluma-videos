At exactly two o'clock, the stopped gold watch started running again.
And Eli Brooks closed his hand around the key.

Last night, a wrong number sent Eli to Pier Nine with a ticking case. Inside: a gold watch, a brass key with the number nine, and a photo of Eli taken before the phone ever rang. A woman in a red coat wants the key. A man in grey says don't give it to her. And a text says the key opens the door to his father.

"No," Eli said.

The woman's smile stayed. But her eyes changed.
"That's a shame," she said softly. "Your father said no too."

Then the lamp over the blue door went out.

In the dark, a hand grabbed Eli's hood. "Run," said the man in grey.

They ran. Down the wet boards, past the cranes, between rusted containers. Behind them, a car engine started. Headlights swept across the rain.

The man in grey pulled Eli into an old boathouse and shut the door. They stood in the dark, breathing hard, listening.
The car rolled past. Slowly. Then it was gone.

Eli turned. "You knew my father."

The man took off his hat. Grey hair. A long scar across his cheek.
"My name is Walter," he said. "Twenty-two years ago, your father and I carried the same case to the same blue door. He went inside. I stayed on the pier. I was a coward, Eli. I've been watching that door ever since."

"Is he alive?"

Walter didn't answer. He took the gold watch from the case and opened the back. Words were engraved inside.
To Daniel. Come home before two.

Daniel Brooks. Eli's father.

"Door number nine isn't on the pier," Walter said. "It's under it."

He pulled back an old tarp. Stone steps, going down into the dark. Water dripping. The smell of salt and rust.

They went down. One flashlight. A long tunnel under the harbor, older than the city above it. Brick walls. Metal doors on both sides, each with a brass number.
Five. Six. Seven. Eight.

Nine.

The door was black iron. A keyhole in the middle, just the right size.

Eli held up the key. His hand was shaking again.

Then, from the other side of the door, someone knocked.
Three slow knocks.

Eli froze. Walter's flashlight was shaking too.

And Eli's phone buzzed.
Same unknown number. One new message.

"Don't open it yet. She's right behind you."

Eli turned around. At the end of the tunnel, in the dark, something red was moving.

The watch in his pocket ticked. Two-oh-four.

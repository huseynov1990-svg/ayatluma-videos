WRONG NUMBER - Season 1, Episode 1: "Locker 14"
Narrator: you. Calm, low, a little tense. About 4 minutes.
Record in one take if you can; pauses between paragraphs are fine.

---

At 1:12 in the morning, a stranger called Eli Brooks by mistake.
By sunrise, three people would be looking for him.

Eli was twenty-four. He delivered food on an old bike with a yellow bag on his back. That night he was sitting in a diner on Fifth Street, waiting for his last order, with a cold coffee and a letter in his pocket. Final notice. Eighteen hundred dollars in rent, or he was out by Friday.

Then his phone rang. Unknown number.

A man's voice. Quiet. Fast.
"You're the courier?"
Eli said, "Yeah... I guess."
"Grand Street Station. Locker fourteen. The code is coming now. Take what's inside to the blue door on Pier Nine. Before two. Five thousand when you arrive. Don't open it. Don't call this number."
And the line went dead.

A second later, a text. Four digits. Seven, three, one, nine.

Eli looked at the screen for a long time. It was the wrong number. He knew it was the wrong number. Somewhere in this city, a real courier was waiting for this call.

But five thousand dollars was almost three months of rent.

He paid for the coffee. He got on the bike. And he rode into the rain.

Grand Street Station was empty at this hour. One guard, asleep. One light flickering over the old lockers.

Locker fourteen was in the middle row.

Seven. Three. One. Nine.

Click.

Inside was a small metal case. Heavy. Cold. With a yellow sticker on the handle. Just one word: "FRAGILE."

Eli picked it up. And that's when he felt it.
The case was humming. Very softly. Like something inside was awake.

Behind him, a door closed.

At the end of the hall stood a man in a long grey coat. He wasn't moving. He was just watching. Watching Eli. Watching the case.

Eli's phone buzzed again. Same number. One new message.
"Don't trust the man in grey."

Eli looked up. The man took one step forward.

Eli ran.

Out of the station, onto the bike, down to the river. Rain in his eyes. The case pressing into his back. He didn't look behind him until he reached the pier.

Pier Nine. The blue door. 1:54 in the morning.

Six minutes left.

Eli stood there, wet and shaking, with the case in his hands. It was still humming. And now he could hear something else, too. Very quiet. From inside.

Ticking.

He could open it. Find out what he was carrying. What he was risking his life for.

Or he could knock on the blue door, take the five thousand, and never think about this night again.
